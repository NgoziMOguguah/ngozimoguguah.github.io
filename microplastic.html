Microplastics Research

Microplastics have become one of the most widespread emerging contaminants in aquatic ecosystems. My research investigates their occurrence, distribution, transport pathways, ecological impacts, and implications for fisheries, biodiversity, food security, and environmental sustainability.

Research Overview

Microplastics are now found throughout marine and freshwater environments, where they interact with sediments, aquatic organisms, and food webs. My research examines their sources, distribution, abundance, characteristics, and ecological consequences using field investigations, laboratory analyses, and interdisciplinary scientific approaches.

Why Microplastics Matter

Because of their small size and persistence, microplastics can be ingested by aquatic organisms, accumulate within ecosystems, and potentially affect biodiversity, fisheries resources, ecosystem functioning, and food safety. Understanding these processes is essential for effective environmental management.

Research Objectives

My microplastics research investigates the occurrence, sources, transport, fate, and ecological impacts of microplastics in aquatic ecosystems. The goal is to generate robust scientific evidence that supports pollution prevention, ecosystem protection, and evidence-based environmental policy.

Occurrence & Distribution

Determine the abundance and spatial distribution of microplastics in water, sediments, fish, and other aquatic organisms across freshwater, estuarine, and marine environments.

Source Identification

Investigate the major sources and transport pathways of microplastics entering aquatic ecosystems through urban runoff, wastewater, industrial activities, and land-based pollution.

Ecological Impact

Assess interactions between microplastics and aquatic organisms, including ingestion, bioaccumulation, habitat quality, and ecosystem functioning.

Science-to-Policy

Provide scientific evidence that supports pollution reduction strategies, plastic waste management, and sustainable environmental governance.

Research Workflow

My investigations follow a structured workflow from field sampling through laboratory analysis and data interpretation to ensure reliable and reproducible scientific results.

1. Field Sampling

Collection of water, sediment, and biological samples from rivers, lagoons, estuaries, and coastal environments.

2. Laboratory Processing

Sample preparation, filtration, density separation, digestion of organic matter, and contamination control following established laboratory protocols.

3. Identification

Characterisation of suspected microplastics based on size, colour, shape, and polymer type using appropriate analytical techniques.

4. Data Analysis

Statistical analysis, GIS mapping, interpretation, and reporting to support research publications and environmental management.

Laboratory Analysis

Sample Preparation

Preparation of environmental samples using standardized laboratory procedures that minimize contamination and improve analytical accuracy.

Microplastic Classification

Classification of particles according to size, colour, morphology, and probable polymer type to understand pollution characteristics.

Quality Assurance

Implementation of quality control procedures, laboratory blanks, duplicate analyses, and contamination prevention throughout the analytical process.

Spatial Analysis

GIS-based mapping of sampling locations and microplastic abundance to identify pollution hotspots and environmental trends.

Ecological Assessment

Evaluation of relationships between microplastic occurrence, environmental quality, and aquatic ecosystem health.

Scientific Communication

Transforming laboratory findings into peer-reviewed publications, policy recommendations, technical reports, and public engagement materials.
